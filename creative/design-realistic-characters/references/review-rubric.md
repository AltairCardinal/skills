# Review rubric

Judge at normal viewing size before zooming into defects. Record each category separately as pass, warning, or fail.

## Identity realism

- Does the person feel like one individual rather than a demographic average?
- Do facial differences relate across regions instead of appearing as random defects?
- Does asymmetry remain subtle and anatomically coherent?
- Are marks localized rather than uniformly distributed texture?
- Would the face remain specific if the background, wardrobe, and color were hidden?

Fail when the face is harmonious but anonymous, waxy, procedurally textured, beautified, or visibly assembled from prompt tokens.

## Character fit

- Does apparent age fit the story stage?
- Does styling fit the person’s ordinary life rather than advertise an archetype?
- Is the character adult, autonomous, and culturally specific without stereotyping?
- Are unwanted codes present, such as villain, hero, victim, idol, influencer, or fashion model?

A realistic face can fail character fit. Create a new identity when the casting is wrong.

## Emotional casting

- Does the neutral person invite the intended amount of social access?
- Is warmth conveyed without a sales smile?
- Is vulnerability possible without a pleading or childlike expression?
- Is menace coming from the intended story role rather than accidental facial coding?

Do not use anatomy as evidence of moral character. Evaluate the combined casting impression and plan for behavior to carry the story.

## Photography

- Does the image resemble an optical photograph rather than a polished render?
- Are focus, exposure, white balance, and texture plausible without becoming “realism effects”?
- Is the face unchanged from the approved anchor?

Do not pass a weak identity because grain, darkness, bokeh, or compression hides it.

## Performance

- Can the viewer name the triggering event rather than only the emotion label?
- Are there only one or two involuntary signals?
- Are hands, mouth, eyebrows, tears, and sweat avoiding redundant explanation?
- Does the person still look like the approved identity mid-reaction?

## Decision outcomes

- **Approve identity:** lock the asset and move to photography.
- **Realistic, wrong casting:** keep as a failed casting sample; design a new identity.
- **Right identity, artificial photograph:** preserve the face; revise photography only.
- **Right person and shot, theatrical acting:** preserve identity and photography; revise the micro-situation and visible response.
- **Persistent synthetic identity:** stop prompt-only retries and request an authorized real-person reference or stronger identity-conditioning workflow.
